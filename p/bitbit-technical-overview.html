What BitBit is

BitBit is not a platform. BitBit is not a tool. BitBit is a thing that works for you.

Think of it like hiring someone who already knows your business. BitBit reads your emails, manages your invoices, triages your messages, follows up with your clients, hunts for tenders, writes your proposals. It knows who Dave is, that he owes you money, and that he mentioned Steve's project in last Tuesday's email. It connects everything and it remembers everything.

Every user gets their own BitBit. You plug in your services, BitBit learns your world, and then it gets to work. You can message it from WhatsApp while you're on a job site. You can call it. You can ignore it and let it handle things. It tells you what it did, not what it's about to do.

The problem

Business operators are buried in admin. Email, calendars, messaging apps, invoicing, CRM. Every service is its own silo. Context about people and relationships lives in twenty different places.

Most AI assistants wait for instructions. They react. BitBit is different because it thinks ahead. When a new email arrives, BitBit has already figured out who sent it, what it's about, which project it relates to, and whether it needs your attention. That understanding was built before you even asked.

How it works (summary)

Connections. One screen with tiles for every service. Gmail, Calendar, WhatsApp, Slack, Xero. Click to connect. No confusing distinction between channels and integrations. It's all just connections.

Context Baseplate. BitBit's compiled understanding of your world. Not the typical search and retrieve approach. BitBit does its thinking when new information arrives, not when you ask a question. It builds an entity graph with relationships, patterns, and active threads.

Autonomous roles. Domain owning roles, not just task executing agents. Finance handles all money operations. Comms handles all communication. Sales handles growth. Each role runs continuously, learns from outcomes, and adapts. You control how much autonomy each role gets: Observer (watch only), Co-pilot (draft and approve), or Autopilot (act and report).

Intelligence layer. Revenue Radar identifies upsell opportunities. Client Health Score rates every relationship 0 to 100. Cash Flow Prophet projects finances forward. Capacity Oracle warns before you overcommit. All computed from real data.

Who it's for

Marketing agencies (first target)

Running multiple client accounts, burning hours on proposals and reporting, losing leads because response time is too slow. Full suite of 10 agents configured for agency work. Lead generation, proposal automation, ad script generation, client onboarding.

Trades and service businesses

Electricians, plumbers, builders. People who do real work during the day and invoicing at night. Message BitBit from your phone while on a job site. Leaner bundle: Sentry, Channel Triage, Invoice Flow, Client Comms.

Enterprise and tender hunting

Companies chasing $10K+ contracts need to find tenders, check eligibility, and respond fast. The Tender Hunter agent is the premium play. $1,000 to $2,000 per month offering.

High touch service businesses

Dentists, physios, accountants. Any business juggling many client relationships, appointments, follow ups, and billing. The more agents relevant to their workflow, the higher the value.

The common thread: all of these people started a business to do the work they're good at. Instead they spend hours every day on admin. BitBit gives them that time back. It's not another tool they have to learn. It's something that learns them.

Technical architecture

Everything below is the full technical walkthrough. Assumes familiarity with LLMs, tool calling, and modern web infrastructure.

1. Message ingestion

BitBit doesn't live inside one app. It connects to the user's actual messaging accounts via per user bridge instances.

ChannelBridge techHostingConnection

Gmail / CalendarComposio OAuthVercel FunctionsStandard OAuth flow

Every message, regardless of source, gets normalised into a universal Envelope format. The rest of the system never thinks about "WhatsApp" or "iMessage." It just processes Envelopes.

Bridge lifecycle is tiered to control costs. Active (bridge running, messages flowing), Suspended (after 7 days idle, bridge hibernated), Destroyed (user disconnects, bridge torn down). A warm pool of 2 pre provisioned Mac VPS instances means new iMessage connections take less than 5 seconds.

2. The brain: TAOR loop

The agent engine is taor-loop.ts (~600 lines). TAOR stands for Think, Act, Observe, Repeat.

Step 1: Pre flight checks

Safety gates. Is the user authenticated? Is the org active? Are confidence thresholds calibrated? Does an entity level delegation mandate exist?

Step 2: Model routing via AI gateway

Not every message needs the biggest model. A model router classifies the query purpose, and a gateway adapter bridges Anthropic SDK types to the Vercel AI SDK's streamText() API.

PurposeModelMax tokensCost (input/output per 1M)
ClassificationGemini 3 Flash4,096$0.15 / $0.60
ConversationClaude Sonnet 4.58,192$3.00 / $15.00
SynthesisClaude Opus 416,384$15.00 / $75.00

All calls go through withCircuitBreaker(), a per key circuit (CLOSED, OPEN, HALF_OPEN) that opens after 5 consecutive failures with a 60s cooldown.

Step 3: System 1/2 query gate

Inspired by Kahneman's dual process theory. Every inbound message is classified before context assembly begins.

System 1 (fast path, <50ms)

Greetings, confirmations, simple lookups, reminders, single action commands. Context assembler runs with maxEntities: 3, no compressed history. About 80% of queries qualify.

System 2 (full retrieval, ~200ms)

Anything involving reasoning, temporal depth, capacity analysis, aggregation, or multi entity reasoning. Gets all 4 context tiers, full compressed history, and proactive memory recall.

Step 4: Context assembly (Total Recall)

This is where BitBit's intelligence actually lives. The ContextAssembler builds a rich system prompt from 4 tiers, all fetched in parallel via Promise.allSettled with per tier timeouts.

TierWhatDegradation
T1: Working memorySystem prompt, identity, guidelines, current date/stateFatal if missing
T2: Session historyRecent turns (verbatim) + compressed older turns + extracted key factsFalls back to no history
T3: Compiled memoryEntity baseplates, proactive memory recall, procedural memories, knowledge graph aliasesGraceful skip
T4: Action statePending approval queue items with confidence scores and timestampsGraceful skip

Token budget manager: Total context budget is 48K tokens (standard) or 200K (dynamic workspace preset). Allocation uses greedy priority based assignment with character ratio heuristics (3.5 chars/token for text, 3.0 for JSON). Target: under 200ms total assembly. Each tier degrades gracefully.

Global Workspace Theory (optional mode)

Replaces fixed tier allocation with competitive module selection. Six memory modules compete for context budget based on query relevance.

Step 5: Tool selection (3 stage pipeline)

BitBit has 60+ tools across 16 groups. Loading all of them into every request wastes tokens and increases model confusion. The solution is a 3 stage pipeline.

  • Haiku planner (planner.ts): A fast, cheap model (Gemini Flash) reads the message and outputs structured JSON with stages, tool groups, complexity, and skills. Runs as a Promise.race with a 1500ms timeout. Also produces a UI visible execution plan streamed to the frontend.
  • Tool RAG (tool-rag.ts): Second pass relevance filtering. Scores tools by keyword overlap, boosts direct name matches, always includes core group, returns top 16.
  • Deferred loading (deferred-loader.ts): Unselected tools listed by name only in the system prompt. The model can call resolve_tool to fetch the full schema on demand. Lazy loading for tool definitions.

    • Tool groups (16 total)

    core — tasks, contacts, invoices, projects, standing orders

    memory — search/store memories, graph traversal, procedural memory

    channel — Gmail/Outlook send, Calendar, inbox search

    web — search, read, extract, crawl, fetch URL, browse

    comms — send email/SMS/WhatsApp, manage approvals

    agentic — execute code, spawn sub agents, async tasks

    ads — video ad scripts, batch management

    seo — AI visibility audit, content, schema markup

    tenders — search government tenders, score fit, generate responses

    content — social posts, blog drafts, content calendar

    builder — generate/revise/deploy/preview websites

    creative — AI image generation via gateway

    composio — 1000+ third party app integrations

    workspace — ephemeral Firecracker microVMs

    browser — cloud browser automation via Stagehand

    escalation — human handoff when automated tiers exhausted

    Step 5b: Skill activation

    Beyond tools, BitBit has Skills, domain specific instruction modules. Each skill has an ID, name, description, tool group, plan gate (subscription tier), and estimated tokens. Skill RAG matches the user's message against the skill index. Up to 2 skills per turn. Skills can contribute their own tool definitions.

    Step 6: The loop itself

    The TAOR loop is an AsyncGenerator<AgentEvent> that yields typed events streamed to the frontend via SSE. The model decides when to stop. Safety ceiling: 50 iterations (configurable via entity overrides). In practice, 1 to 3 iterations.

    19 distinct event types: thinking progress, lifecycle stages, execution plans, tool calls and results, streaming text, citations, sub agent lifecycle, cost/budget events, and terminal done.

    3. Autonomy and confidence routing

    Three autonomy levels

    Observer

    Watches and surfaces insights. Never acts.

    Co-pilot

    Drafts actions and queues them for approval.

    Autopilot

    Acts independently within confidence thresholds.

    Confidence router

    Every action has a confidence score. The router compares it against thresholds resolved via cascade: calibrated thresholds (from historical data), agent level config, agent type defaults, org settings, global defaults (act: 0.85, ask: 0.55).

    Agent typeAct thresholdAsk thresholdRisk level
    invoice-flow0.920.60High (money leaves the business)
    client-comms0.880.58High (sends as the user)
    proposal-bot0.900.60High (financial proposals)
    lead-swarm0.850.55Medium
    channel-triage0.800.50Medium (routing)
    tender-hunter0.800.50Medium (sourcing)
    sentry0.750.45Low (alerting only)
    ad-script-gen0.780.48Low (reversible content)

    Confidence ≥ act threshold: execute automatically. Between ask and act: queue for approval (user sees pending actions, can approve conversationally). Below ask: decline gracefully.

    Infinite delegation

    Users can activate or revoke delegation in natural language. "Put Steve on autopilot" and "I'll handle Steve myself" are first class operations. Detection uses regex banks (9 activation patterns, 9 revocation patterns). When active, the confidence router short circuits, and the entity gets elevated iteration cap and cost budget via LTV multiplier.

    4. Memory: the Memory Palace

    BitBit doesn't forget. The Memory Palace is a typed, confidence weighted memory system backed by Supabase with full text search.

    Memory types: conversation, decision, pattern, fact, relationship, pricing, lesson_learned
    Sources: extraction (pulled from messages), user_explicit (user said "remember this"), agent_reflection (BitBit's own conclusions)
    Decay rates: never (promoted conventions), slow (facts), medium (patterns), fast (unproven plans)

    Each memory has a confidence score (0 to 1) that decays over time, gets boosted by corroboration, and penalised by contradiction. Entity links connect memories to contacts, projects, invoices.

    Contradiction detection (real time)

    The TAOR loop tracks which memory IDs were loaded into context for the last response. If the user's next message matches correction patterns ("no, ...", "that's wrong", "actually, ..."), all surfaced memories are penalised by 0.15 confidence. Self correcting knowledge without explicit feedback.

    Procedural memory (learned workflows)

    Each procedure has a trigger pattern (regex), ordered steps, a success count, and a source. When a message arrives, it's tested against all active procedures. If matched, the procedure's steps are injected into context so BitBit follows the learned workflow automatically.

    Sleep consolidation (nightly 7 stage pipeline)

    Runs as a daily cron at 3am UTC. Modelled on human memory consolidation during sleep.

    StageOperationDetail
    1SummarisePer entity daily digest, compress episodic memories into entity level summaries
    1.5Promote patternsPatterns with confidence ≥ 0.7 and sample_count ≥ 3 promoted to permanent conventions
    2Resolve conflictsTemporal precedence for duplicate edges, newer evidence wins
    3Discover relationshipsDetect latent edges from co occurring events
    3.5Detect communitiesCluster entities with mutual edges and shared events
    4PruneArchive low confidence, entity less memories
    5Morning briefingUpcoming deadlines, blocked entities, new discoveries, pending approvals
    6System learningAnalyse turn quality scores, track tool efficiency, context utilisation, consolidation precision
    7Fiduciary evaluationGame Theory LTV analysis, generate per entity fiduciary constraints

    5. Entity graph: the world model

    BitBit maintains an entity relationship graph in Postgres. Contact, Project, Invoice, Task, Goal, Channel Message, all connected.

    Each relationship has a type (e.g. "owns", "works_on", "billed_for"), strength (0 to 1, evidence weighted), and last evidence timestamp.

    When you ask "How's Steve's project going?", it doesn't just search. It traverses the graph: Steve, projects, tasks, recent activity, invoices, payment status, then composes a holistic answer.

    Dual embeddings (OpenAI 768d + Voyage 1024d) enable both keyword and semantic search across entities.

    6. Tool execution

    When the model decides to call a tool, execution goes through layers:

  • Model emits tool_use
  • Plan gate check (is this tool allowed on the user's plan?)
  • Role budget check (has the ads budget been exceeded this month?)
  • Execution token cap (prevent runaway compute)
  • Actual handler execution
  • Result truncation (max 12K chars)
  • JIT instruction injection (tells the model how to present the result)
  • Citation extraction (for source attribution)
  • Action reflection (logs what happened for future context)

    • JIT instructions are a pattern where after each tool returns, BitBit injects a one line instruction telling the model how to present the result. Example: after generate_invoice, the instruction says "Invoice rendered as an artifact. Do NOT repeat the details as text."

    Sub agents

    For complex tasks with multiple independent action chains, the engine spawns sub agents with their own TAOR loops and context windows. Sub agents start fresh and can be scoped to specific tool groups. Depth guard prevents infinite recursion.

    Ephemeral workspaces

    Firecracker microVM sandboxes via E2B for code execution and data analysis. Templates: default, data science (pandas/numpy/matplotlib), web dev (Node.js). 5 minute auto destroy. Full lifecycle: budget check, create, store, execute, deliver output, complete.

    Browser automation

    Cloud browser via Stagehand SDK + Browserbase. Self healing navigation with 3 tier element targeting: cached selector (near instant), DOM/accessibility inference (fast), vision with screenshot and CUA model (slower). Accepts natural language task descriptions.

    Turn quality evaluator

    After each successful TAOR run, a fire and forget call scores the turn on three dimensions (0.0 to 1.0): tool efficiency (minimum tools needed?), context utilisation (were surfaced memories actually referenced?), and confidence calibration (appropriately assertive?). Scores aggregate nightly to tune thresholds.

    9. Data flow: end to end

    What happens when someone WhatsApps a BitBit user:

  • WhatsApp message hits mautrix bridge, flows through Matrix, arrives as webhook POST to Vercel
  • Provider plugin normalises to Envelope: sender, body, timestamp, channel, metadata
  • Envelope hits TAOR loop entry. Pre flight (auth, org check, delegation mandates), model routing (classify and select model), System 1/2 gate
  • Context assembly (under 200ms, parallel): system prompt, conversation history, entity baseplates, pending approvals, proactive memory recall
  • Tool selection: Haiku planner selects tool groups (1500ms race), Tool RAG filters to top 16, Skill RAG activates domain skills
  • TAOR loop: Think (call Claude with full context + tools), Act (execute tool calls in parallel with safety checks), Observe (append results, inject JIT instructions), Repeat (model decides if more iterations needed)
  • Response guard scrubs internal engine details and model identity leaks. Stream final response via SSE.
  • Post processing: log agent run (tokens, cost, duration), store plan as memory, record action outcomes for confidence calibration, turn quality evaluation

    • 10. What makes this different

    Persistent world model with self improvement

    The entity graph + Memory Palace + sleep consolidation pipeline means BitBit accumulates institutional knowledge. Pattern promotion turns repeated observations into permanent conventions. Contradiction detection self corrects mistakes. The turn quality evaluator closes the loop, measuring its own performance and tuning itself nightly.

    Graduated autonomy with natural language control

    The confidence router + approval queue + delegation mandates create a trust gradient that users control conversationally. Per entity LTV multipliers scale the cost budget proportional to relationship value.

    Multi channel native with unified context

    BitBit is inside the user's actual WhatsApp, iMessage, Gmail, and Calendar via per user bridge instances. Cross channel continuity is structural, not retrieval based. It knows the WhatsApp message from Steve relates to the Gmail thread from last week and the invoice that's 30 days overdue.

    Cognitive architecture, not just tool calling

    The System 1/2 query gate, Global Workspace competitive memory allocation, complexity gated extended thinking, and 3 stage tool selection pipeline mean every request gets precisely the resources it needs. This is a deliberately layered cognitive architecture with measurable latency and cost budgets at every stage.

    Composable capability surface

    60+ tools, 16 tool groups, domain skills, procedural memories, Composio integrations (1000+ apps), ephemeral workspaces, and browser automation, all dynamically selected per query. New capabilities plug in without touching the engine.

    11. Integration layer: Composio + MCP

    For everything beyond core business operations (HubSpot, Notion, Jira, Salesforce, Slack, Google Sheets, etc.), BitBit uses Composio, a managed OAuth + action platform for 1000+ apps.

    Native Composio tools let the agent browse available apps, discover actions, execute them, and initiate OAuth connections. When MCP mode is enabled, third party app actions are exposed as standard tool definitions and merged into the TAOR loop's tool array. The engine deduplicates by name so a Composio action never shadows a native tool.
